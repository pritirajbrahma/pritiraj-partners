The National Capital Territory of Delhi

Seventy Assembly seats, seven Lok Sabha seats, and a legislature that does not control its police or its land. Delhi is where India argues about who owns delivery.

Delhi is two terrains sharing a pincode: the seat of the Union, where policy is written, and a state government asked to deliver services it does not fully control.

The arithmetic

70 Assembly constituencies; 7 Lok Sabha seats. The Assembly last went to the polls in February 2025. Delhi's electorate splits its verdict between national and state elections more sharply than almost any other, which tells a strategist something exact: the voter is distinguishing between the promise of a nation and the delivery of a city, and is willing to reward different parties for each.

The competence question

Under Article 239AA, the Legislative Assembly of the National Capital Territory does not legislate on public order, police or land. The Government of NCT of Delhi (Amendment) Act, 2021 and the further amendment of 2023 on services settled — by statute, after litigation — where authority over officers sits. Municipal government was consolidated by the Delhi Municipal Corporation (Amendment) Act, 2022, folding three corporations back into one.

The practical result is a city where one street can be the responsibility of some combination of the MCD, the NDMC, the DDA, the Delhi Jal Board, the Public Works Department and the Delhi Police, answering to two different elected mandates. This is the purest form of the firm's thesis: not an absence of money or of intent, but an absence of a single named owner.

Delhi as the policy capital

The other Delhi mandate has nothing to do with the NCT. Union ministries, the Finance Commission, the regulators and the parliamentary committees are here, and a state government's case for its own share of resources is won or lost in this city. The firm does that work as a delivery argument built on published instruments — a state's own audited numbers, a CAG finding, a parliamentary reply — rather than as access.

What a mandate here involves

  1. Mapping authority before designing delivery. In Delhi the first deliverable is an honest table of who actually owns each function.
  2. Campaign arguments that separate the two mandates. The Delhi voter already does this. A strategy that does not is arguing against the electorate's own literacy.
  3. Union-facing cases built from documents. Attributed figures, not relationships.
