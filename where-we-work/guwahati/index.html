Guwahati

The North East's only genuine metropolitan market, and the city through which the region's administration, media and money all pass. What is decided or reported in Guwahati reaches all eight states.

Guwahati is where the firm keeps its North East practice. Three decades of the region's politics have been argued, reported and administered from this city, and a firm that does not sit inside that traffic is reading the region at second hand.

What the city decides

Guwahati is the region's administrative gateway, its media centre and its only metropolitan consumer market. Assam's seat of government sits at Dispur inside the city's limits. The regional bureaux of every national outlet are here, as is the Assamese-language press that sets the day's frame for a readership no Delhi newsroom reaches. A campaign that wins Guwahati's coverage has not won a state; a campaign that loses it has lost the ability to explain itself.

The city's own Assembly segments were redrawn along with the rest of Assam in the Election Commission's 2023 delimitation — the first since 1976. Seat counts across the state did not change; boundaries did. Every historical margin and every claim to a home ground was rebased in a single order, and a strategist still working from pre-2023 constituency data in and around this city is working from a map that no longer exists.

What we hold here

The firm has won mandates in Assam, Meghalaya and Arunachal Pradesh, and is engaged in Nagaland. That work leaves something behind: booth-level results, constituency histories, community and language maps, candidate records and a standing set of field relationships across the eight states — maintained continuously rather than assembled for a pitch. It is the single reason a mandate in this region does not begin with a month of orientation.

Terrain knowledge in the North East is not a claim about coverage. It is knowing which authority actually delivers in a given block, which language a household argues in, which council election is running on its own cycle, and which convening body — a village council, a students' union, a church — has to be in the room before anything is announced.

The delivery question

The Guwahati Municipal Corporation carries a city whose flood, drainage and traffic failures are annual and thoroughly documented. They are not mysterious: the hills that drain into the city, the wetlands that used to absorb the water, the arterial roads and the corporation's own works sit under different authorities, and no single officer is required to publish a number for the whole system. This is the firm's thesis at municipal scale — not an absence of money or of engineering, but an absence of one named owner.

What a mandate here involves

  1. Rebasing everything to the 2023 boundaries. Non-negotiable, and the most common error outside firms make in Assam.
  2. Working the Assamese-language press as the primary channel. English coverage out of Guwahati reaches Delhi. Assamese coverage reaches the voter.
  3. Language and community literacy as a working requirement. Assamese, Bodo, Bengali and the tea-garden constituencies are not a segmentation exercise; they are the terrain.
  4. Municipal delivery instruments with a single owner. For the flood and drainage question, the deliverable is a published monthly number and an officer whose name is on it.
