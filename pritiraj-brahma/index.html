How the Review is made

The Review is a by-product of the firm's practice, not a separate research operation. That is the entire explanation for its cadence.

Where the essays come from. Reading the ground picture on government initiatives is the firm's daily work — what was announced, what was sanctioned, what reached the district. That reading runs continuously and produces a daily note for the leaders the firm advises. An essay begins only where a pattern in those notes has held across states and across years, and the evidence for it is already on the record. The research precedes the essay; the essay does not commission the research.

The evidentiary standard. Four rules govern every figure the Review prints.

  1. Every figure is traced to a named source before it runs. The primary document read directly where one exists — a budget, an audit, a judgment, a Finance Commission volume — and otherwise an official statistical release from MoSPI, the RBI or the NSS, a PIB or ministry note, or a parliamentary question and its reply. Each essay closes by naming the institutions its evidence comes from.
  2. Two independent sources wherever practicable. A figure that appears once and nowhere else is treated as a claim, not a fact, and is either corroborated or dropped.
  3. Disagreement is reported, not resolved silently. Where credible sources conflict, the essay carries both numbers and says which it relies on and why. Choosing one quietly is the more common practice and the less honest one.
  4. Reporting is labelled as reporting. Where no primary source is public, the figure is attributed to the publication that carried it and identified as such. Nothing is estimated, rounded toward the argument, or carried from memory.

What tools are involved. The arguments and the words are the author's. AI tools assist in structuring an argument once its evidence is assembled, and built this site and its pages. They are not used to source facts. No figure enters an essay on their authority: every number is verified by the author against the document it came from. Where the author has an interest in the subject of an essay, that essay carries a disclosure line.

Corrections. A publication that demands accountability of the state owes the same standard. An error here will be corrected, the essay marked as revised with the date, and the correction will say what changed rather than quietly replacing it. Every essay carries its publication and last-revision date under the dek, and a data-vintage note where the argument rests on a dated series. Corrections and challenges reach the author through the channels above.
