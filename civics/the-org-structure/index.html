The org structure of the Union and a state

The Union has a head of state, a head of government, a council of ministers and two Houses. Every state repeats the shape with different names. This is that structure, drawn once, with the constitutional article that creates each office.

Two structures, one shape: the Union at the centre, and every state repeating it under different names.

01

The lower House

543 members are directly elected from single-member constituencies under Article 81, against a constitutional ceiling of 550. Money bills can begin only here, and the government holds office only while this House sustains it.

ARTICLES 81 · 110

02

The upper House

245 members: 233 elected by the state legislatures under Article 80 and 12 nominated for distinction in letters, science, art and social service. It never dissolves — a third retires every two years.

ARTICLE 80 · CEILING 250

03

The ministry

The Council of Ministers is capped at 15 per cent of the strength of the Lok Sabha, in three ranks — Cabinet Minister, Minister of State, Deputy Minister. The cap was written in by the 91st Amendment.

How India is governed

Two parallel structures. The Union government at the centre, and the same pattern repeated in every state. Elected legislators sit on the right of each half; the executive they sustain sits on the left.
Directly elected  MPs, MLAs
Appointed  Governor, civil service
Political executive  PM, CM, ministers
Permanent executive  secretaries, collectors

A state

The same pattern, one level down — the Chief Minister's government
Legislature
Source of authority
The state's electorate
State election every five years, held separately from the general election.
Lower House
Legislative Assembly
MLAs
Vidhan Sabha. Members directly elected from constituencies within the state; strength between 60 and 500 depending on population. Sustains or dismisses the Chief Minister.
Upper House — only in six states
Legislative Council
MLCs
Vidhan Parishad. Where it exists, no larger than a third of the Assembly. Members are elected by local bodies, teachers and graduates, by MLAs, or nominated by the Governor.
