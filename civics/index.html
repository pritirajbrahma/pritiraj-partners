Politics is the promise. Governance is the delivery. Most argument about Indian government skips the part where you have to know what the machinery actually is — so here it is, in five charts, in the order they answer each other.

Roles only, no officeholders named, so it stays accurate between elections.

01

Who it is for

Anyone who votes and wants to know where that vote actually lands — students, first-time voters, aspirants preparing for the services, and readers who were never shown the whole structure in one place.

FREE TO USE, ALWAYS

02

Why we publish it

The firm’s essays argue about how India is governed. They presuppose a reader who knows how it is built. That literacy should not be a paywall or a textbook chapter — so we publish it, free, and argue separately.

LITERACY FIRST, ARGUMENT AFTER

03

How to read it

In order, File 01 to 05. Each answers a question the one before it raises: structure, then who leads it, then how it makes law, then how a citizen enters it, then why part of it is never elected.
